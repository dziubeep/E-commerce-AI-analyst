# ── Q1: Total Revenue ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Question: What is the total revenue?
Answer:
SELECT ROUND(SUM(total_sales), 2) AS total_revenue
FROM orders;
Explanation: I calculated total revenue by summing the total_sales column, which represents quantity × price for every order line.


# ── Q2: Revenue for a Specific Month ─────────────────────────────────────────
Question: What was the revenue in November 2011?
Answer:
SELECT ROUND(SUM(total_sales), 2) AS monthly_revenue
FROM orders
WHERE strftime('%Y-%m', invoicedate) = '2011-11';
Explanation: I filtered orders to November 2011 using strftime on the invoicedate column and summed total_sales.


# ── Q3: Top 10 Products by Revenue ───────────────────────────────────────────
Question: What are the top 10 best-selling products by revenue?
Answer:
SELECT description,
       ROUND(SUM(total_sales), 2) AS revenue
FROM orders
GROUP BY description
ORDER BY revenue DESC
LIMIT 10;
Explanation: I grouped all order lines by product description, summed revenue for each, and returned the top 10 in descending order.


# ── Q4: Revenue by Country ───────────────────────────────────────────────────
Question: Show me revenue broken down by country.
Answer:
SELECT country,
       ROUND(SUM(total_sales), 2) AS revenue
FROM orders
GROUP BY country
ORDER BY revenue DESC;
Explanation: I grouped orders by country and summed total_sales for each, sorted from highest to lowest revenue.


# ── Q5: Average Order Value ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
Question: What is the average order value?
Answer:
SELECT ROUND(SUM(total_sales) / COUNT(DISTINCT invoice), 2) AS avg_order_value
FROM orders;
Explanation: I calculated Average Order Value (AOV) by dividing total revenue by the number of unique invoices. Each invoice represents one customer transaction.


# ── Q6: Total Number of Unique Customers ─────────────────────────────────────
Question: How many unique customers do we have?
Answer:
SELECT COUNT(DISTINCT customer_id) AS unique_customers
FROM orders;
Explanation: I counted the distinct customer IDs across all orders to find the total number of unique customers.


# ── Q7: Monthly Revenue Trend ────────────────────────────────────────────────
Question: Show me the monthly revenue trend.
Answer:
SELECT strftime('%Y-%m', invoicedate) AS month,
       ROUND(SUM(total_sales), 2)     AS revenue
FROM orders
GROUP BY month
ORDER BY month;
Explanation: I grouped orders by year-month using strftime and summed revenue for each period, sorted chronologically. This shows how revenue changes over time.

# ── Q9: Repeat Customer Rate ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
Question: What percentage of customers are repeat buyers?
Answer:
SELECT ROUND(
         100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN order_count > 1 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)
         / COUNT(*), 2
       ) AS repeat_customer_pct
FROM (
    SELECT customer_id, COUNT(DISTINCT invoice) AS order_count
    FROM orders
    GROUP BY customer_id
);
Explanation: I first counted unique invoices per customer. Then I calculated what percentage of customers had more than one invoice — those are repeat buyers.


# ── Q10: Revenue by Day of Week ──────────────────────────────────────────────
Question: Which day of the week generates the most revenue?
Answer:
SELECT CASE CAST(strftime('%w', invoicedate) AS INTEGER)
           WHEN 0 THEN 'Sunday'
           WHEN 1 THEN 'Monday'
           WHEN 2 THEN 'Tuesday'
           WHEN 3 THEN 'Wednesday'
           WHEN 4 THEN 'Thursday'
           WHEN 5 THEN 'Friday'
           WHEN 6 THEN 'Saturday'
       END AS day_of_week,
       ROUND(SUM(total_sales), 2) AS revenue
FROM orders
GROUP BY day_of_week
ORDER BY revenue DESC;
Explanation: I extracted the day of the week from each invoice date using strftime('%w'), mapped it to a readable name, and summed revenue for each day.


# ── Q11: Average Revenue by Day of Week ──────────────────────────────────────

# ── Q12: Products Most Frequently Bought Together ────────────────────────────
Question: Which products are most frequently bought together on the same invoice?
Answer:
SELECT a.description AS product_a,
       b.description AS product_b,
       COUNT(*)      AS times_bought_together
FROM orders a
JOIN orders b
  ON a.invoice = b.invoice
 AND a.stockcode < b.stockcode
GROUP BY a.description, b.description
ORDER BY times_bought_together DESC
LIMIT 10;
Explanation: I calculated market basket pairs by self-joining the orders table on the same invoice, ensuring each pair is counted once (using stockcode < stockcode). The result shows the top 10 product pairs most often purchased together.


# ── Q13: Top Customers with Days Since Last Purchase ─────────────────────────
Question: Who are the top 10 customers based on total revenue, and how many days since their last purchase?
Answer:
SELECT customer_id,
       ROUND(SUM(total_sales), 2) AS total_revenue,
       COUNT(DISTINCT invoice)    AS num_orders,
       MAX(invoicedate)           AS last_purchase,
       CAST(julianday('now') - julianday(MAX(invoicedate)) AS INTEGER) AS days_since_last_purchase
FROM orders
GROUP BY customer_id
ORDER BY total_revenue DESC
LIMIT 10;
Explanation: I calculated total revenue per customer, found their most recent purchase date, and used julianday() to compute how many days have passed since that last purchase.


# ── Q14: High Order Count but Low Revenue Items ─────────────────────────────
Question: Which 10 items have the highest number of total orders but generate the lowest total revenue?
Answer:
SELECT description,
       COUNT(DISTINCT invoice) AS total_orders,
       ROUND(SUM(total_sales), 2) AS total_revenue
FROM orders
GROUP BY description
ORDER BY total_orders DESC, total_revenue ASC
LIMIT 10;
Explanation: I calculated the number of distinct invoices (orders) and total revenue for each product, then sorted by highest order count first and lowest revenue second. These are high-volume but low-value items.
